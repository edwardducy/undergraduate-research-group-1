# Not Found

This file lists the papers that we could not extract into a note. The list comes from the literature searches of August 2026. Each paper is paywalled or only available as a web page, so Marker could not convert it.

### The Missing Papers

1. Multi-Task Learning Model for Fine-Grained Categorization of Emergency Tweets. This paper appears in IEEE (document 11368070). The authors include Nandan Pradhan and colleagues at Technological University Dublin. The link is https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11368070

2. Attention-based Bilateral LSTM-CNN for the Sentiment Analysis of Code-mixed Filipino-English Social Media Texts. This paper appears in IEEE (document 10248926). The authors are Reyjohn R. Frias and colleagues, 2023. The link is https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10248926/

3. Sentiment Analysis of Code-Switched Filipino-English Product and Service Reviews Using Transformers-Based Large Language Models. This paper is a Springer chapter in the Proceedings of World Conference on Information Systems for Business Management (ISBM 2023). The authors are Camilla Johnine Cosme and Marlene M. De Leon. The DOI is 10.1007/978-981-99-8349-0_11, and the chapter appears in the Ateneo de Manila University Research Portal (publication 411). The links are https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-99-8349-0_11 and https://archium.ateneo.edu/discs-faculty-pubs/411/

### Why These Papers Are Missing

The two IEEE papers sit behind a paywall. Automated tools cannot pass the Springer client challenge, so the Cosme and De Leon chapter is not downloadable either. A fourth paper, Leveraging Multi-Task Learning in Code-Switched setting (Research Square preprint, Mithun Kumar S R and Omkar Pitale), was also missing at first. It is now extracted and lives in the folder Leveraging Multi-Task Learning in Code-Switched setting.

### What These Papers Contain

1. The IEEE emergency categorization paper trains a multi-task model on tweets from the CrisisNLP benchmark datasets. The abstract reports that multi-task learning beats single-task learning on accuracy and F1. This paper is a close English analog to our study, so it matters for the gap section.
2. The IEEE Filipino-English sentiment paper combines a CNN and a bidirectional LSTM with an attention mechanism. It classifies code-mixed Filipino-English texts from social networking sites and synthetic data. It is a Taglish-adjacent study.
3. The Cosme and De Leon chapter introduces FiReCS, a sentiment-annotated corpus of Filipino-English reviews with code-switching, with more than 10,000 instances of product and service reviews. It benchmarks transformer-based large language models and reports zero-shot results with the GPT-3 series. The FiReCS corpus matters for the gap section, because it is a Filipino-English code-switched dataset, though it covers reviews and not disasters.
