# Section Definitions

## Chapter 1

| Section | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1.1 Introduction to the Study | A short opening that establishes the research area, identifies the gap, and states what the study does to fill it |
| 1.2 Background of the Study | The context and prior work in more detail, showing the problem and the gap in existing work that the study addresses |
| 1.3 Statement of the Problem | The general purpose of the study and the specific research questions it answers |
| 1.4 Objectives | What the study sets out to do, one objective for each research question |
| 1.5 Hypotheses | Testable predictions for the research questions, stated as null and alternative hypotheses and tested statistically |
| 1.6 Theoretical Framework | The established theory the study builds on and how it explains the relationships the study expects |
| 1.7 Conceptual Framework | The key concepts of the study and the relationships assumed among them, usually shown as a diagram |
| 1.8 Scope, Limitations, and Delimitations | What the study covers, its weaknesses, and what it deliberately leaves out |
| 1.8.1 Scope of the Study | What the study covers, such as its data, time period, tasks, and models |
| 1.8.2 Limitations of the Study | The weaknesses of the study outside the researchers' control and how each may affect the results |
| 1.8.3 Delimitations of the Study | The boundaries the researchers deliberately set, with the reason for each |
| 1.9 Significance of the Study | Who benefits from the results and what each gains, for research, practice, and policy |
| 1.10 Definitions of Terms | The operational meaning of key terms, as the study uses and measures them |

## Chapter 2

| Section | Definition |
|---|---|
| 2.1 Natural Language Processing of Disaster Posts | How earlier studies and systems classified disaster posts and extracted their key details, and what is missing for Taglish posts |
| 2.2 Multi-Task Learning | The idea the study builds on, the problem it measures, and the methods it compares |
| 2.3 Algorithmic Literature Taxonomy Matrix | A table that compares the reviewed studies and this study side by side on the same concepts |
| 2.4 Synthesis of Related Literature and Studies | The trends across the reviewed studies and the gaps the study fills, linked to the research questions |

## Chapter 3

| Section | Definition |
|---|---|
| 3.1 Research Methodology | The research design and how its activities answer the research questions |
| 3.2 Subjects of the Study | What the study is about, here the dataset and the models, with the criteria for including them |
| 3.3 Sampling Technique | How the sample is drawn from the population, the inclusion and exclusion criteria, how the sample size is justified, and how the data are split |
| 3.4 Instruments to Be Used | The definition of every measure, how it is produced, and the evidence that it is reliable and valid. Full instruments, such as the annotation guidelines, go in an appendix |
| 3.5 Procedure of Data Gathering | How and when the data are collected, and how the experiments are run, including training settings, runs, and seeds |
| 3.6 Statistical Treatment | The inferential tests, how uncertainty is reported (repeated runs, confidence intervals, effect sizes), and the decision rules based on them |
| 3.7 Data Analysis | How the measured values are computed, summarized, and interpreted, beyond the statistical tests |
| 3.8 Software System Description | The design of the software built in the study, described as views of its structure and behavior |
| 3.8.1 System Structure | The parts of the system and how data moves between them |
| 3.8.2 Use Cases | The actors and what each does with the system |
| 3.8.3 Materials and Equipment | The hardware, software, and versions needed to repeat the study |
| 3.9 Testing and Evaluation | How the software is verified against its requirements, and how the artifact is evaluated |
