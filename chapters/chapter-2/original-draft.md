# **Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature and Studies**

This chapter reviews related literature and studies that ground the proposed benchmarking of dynamic multi-task balancing on Taglish disaster tweets. Following the required thematic organization, the review arranges prior work by computational sub-concept, algorithmic paradigm, and empirical bottleneck rather than by geographical origin.

Section 2.1 examines crisis informatics corpora, the evidence for joint multi-task modeling of Named Entity Recognition (NER), intent classification, and urgency classification, and the computational and statistical standards that such modeling must satisfy. Section 2.2 analyzes multi-task optimization (MTO) dynamics, covering dynamic loss-weighting methods, gradient-surgery and projection techniques, and the empirical evidence questioning whether specialized balancing algorithms outperform static scalarization. Section 2.3 reviews code-switched text processing, subword tokenization disparities, and Philippine language resources, including local disaster response studies. Section 2.4 consolidates key studies into an Algorithmic Literature Taxonomy Matrix positioned against the proposed research. Section 2.5 presents a three-step synthesis that derives the research gap and articulates how the proposed study bridges that gap. The reviewed corpus draws on peer-reviewed sources indexed in the ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, and the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL) Anthology, identified through database searches on multi-task optimization, crisis informatics, and code-switching conducted as of 2026, with the majority of sources published within the last five years.

## **2.1 Joint Multi-Task Modeling and Computational Efficiency in Crisis Informatics**

### **2.1.1 Crisis Informatics Corpora and Benchmark Protocols**

Crisis informatics research established that human-annotated social media corpora enable supervised processing of disaster messages at operational scales. Imran et al. (2016) collected over 52 million crisis-related tweets across 19 disaster events and released approximately 50,000 human-annotated messages spanning nine United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UN OCHA) humanitarian categories. Across Naive Bayes, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine classifiers, the corpora achieved Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) scores of at least 0.80 for most humanitarian categories, while the rare "missing, trapped, or found people" category suffered measurably lower performance because of severe class imbalance. The authors additionally documented five categories of out-of-vocabulary noise in crisis microblogs, including misspellings, abbreviations, and phonetic substitutions, confirming that informal disaster text requires specialized lexical handling.

Subsequent benchmarks standardized label taxonomies, deduplication, and evaluation splits. Alam et al. (2021a) consolidated eight crisis tweet corpora into CrisisBench and demonstrated that exact and near-duplicate messages constitute 20 to 25 percent of uncleaned crisis datasets, so that duplicate removal prevents data leakage between training and evaluation splits. On the consolidated humanitarian task, RoBERTa achieved a weighted F1-score of 0.872 compared with 0.829 for a convolutional neural network baseline, and multilingual evaluation exhibited a performance drop of approximately 8 percentage points relative to English-only benchmarks. Alam et al. (2021b) released HumAID, a 77,196-tweet corpus covering 19 disaster events between 2016 and 2019, filtered from 24.8 million raw tweets through geographic, relevance, and near-duplicate pruning, with crowdsourced annotation reaching an average Fleiss' kappa of 0.55. The HumAID benchmark showed that monolingual RoBERTa attained the highest average weighted F1-score of 0.781, followed by multilingual XLM-RoBERTa at 0.777, whereas classical classifiers remained below 0.72. Notably, DistilBERT matched full BERT performance (0.769 versus 0.768 average weighted F1) with substantially fewer parameters, a result the authors identified as directly relevant to latency-sensitive operational deployment.

Recent resources extend document-level corpora toward token-level annotations and multi-task formulations. Ali et al. (2026) constructed HumAID-NER by extending 60,000 English HumAID tweets with approximately 175,000 entity spans across 10 operational entity types and trained a joint RoBERTa-large model for concurrent NER and event classification. The ablation results empirically demonstrated gradient asymmetry in joint training: because token-level NER contributes up to 2,688 supervision signals per sample while event classification contributes a single pooled signal, classification macro F1 degraded from a peak of 0.748 to 0.734 by the final epoch under fixed equal weighting, whereas homoscedastic uncertainty weighting stabilized classification macro F1 at 0.761 alongside an NER span micro F1 of 0.841. In parallel, Wang et al. (2021) formulated disaster triage as multi-task learning on the TREC Incident Streams corpus (40,459 tweets across 42 crisis events), jointly predicting 25 information types and a continuous priority score. The shared BERT encoder outperformed single-task learning on ranking quality (NDCG 0.5101 versus 0.4393) and alerting worth (−0.2689 versus −0.4057 for high-priority alerts), and the encoder ablation showed that parameter-efficient variants (DistilBERT at 66 million parameters and ALBERT at 11 million parameters) remained competitive with full 110-million-parameter encoders. However, the authors explicitly deferred evaluation of computational efficiency, inference latency, and high-throughput deployment to future work.

### **2.1.2 Joint Versus Single-Task Modeling of Token-Level and Sequence-Level Tasks**

Foundational work in multi-task learning (MTL) established the representational and statistical rationale for joint training. Caruana (1997) formalized hard parameter sharing, in which related tasks train in parallel over a common hidden representation, and demonstrated on road-following and medical risk prediction domains that the inductive transfer improves generalization on the primary task. Baxter (2000) proved statistically that learning a shared representation across K related tasks reduces the per-task sample complexity by a factor proportional to 1/K, provided that tasks originate from a common environment distribution. Ruder (2017) systematized these results for deep networks, delineating hard from soft parameter sharing and identifying five inductive transfer mechanisms, including implicit data augmentation, eavesdropping, and representation bias, while flagging the unresolved problem of predicting task compatibility and mitigating conflicting gradients. Zhang and Yang (2021) and Chen et al. (2024) confirmed through broad surveys that deep multi-task models dominate natural language processing and computer vision applications, while simultaneously identifying negative transfer from unrelated or outlier tasks as a persistent theoretical weakness lacking principled mitigation.

Empirical studies of joint sequence labeling and sequence classification provide direct precedent for the proposed NER, intent, and urgency task bundle. Wang et al. (2019) introduced the GLUE multi-task benchmark and reported that multi-task training with attention-based BiLSTM encoders reached a 70.0 macro-average versus 67.4 for single-task counterparts, although diagnostic probing revealed destructive interference when tasks were trained jointly under uniform sampling. Liu et al. (2019b) demonstrated with MT-DNN that multi-task fine-tuning of BERT raised the GLUE benchmark score to 82.7 (versus 80.5 for BERT-large) and enabled domain adaptation with 82.1 percent accuracy on SNLI using only 0.1 percent of in-domain samples, attributing the gains to MTL-induced regularization. In task-oriented dialogue, Weld et al. (2022) surveyed joint intent detection and slot filling and found that joint models consistently outperformed independent pipelines by capturing mutual conditional distributions and eliminating cascading error propagation, with slot F1 on ATIS improving from 93.96 (2014) to 98.75 (2019) and intent accuracy exceeding 99 percent. Weld et al. (2022) cautioned, however, that ATIS and SNIPS have reached saturation, so that benchmark scores measure dataset difficulty rather than language understanding, and they identified standardized training budgets and out-of-domain generalization as open problems. These findings jointly support the premise that pairing token-level NER with sequence-level intent and urgency heads offers strong mutual reinforcement, while implying that the benefits of pairing depend on task relatedness, dataset scale, and optimization control.

### **2.1.3 Complexity Analysis, Statistical Rigor, and Reproducibility Infrastructure**

The computational case for hard parameter sharing follows from asymptotic complexity analysis. Deploying K independent single-task models requires K forward passes per input and O(K⋅|θbackbone|) parameter storage, whereas a shared encoder computes all task features in one forward pass with task heads contributing less than one percent of total parameters, yielding effective 𝒪(1) time and memory relative to task count K (Caruana, 1997; Ruder, 2017; Zhang & Yang, 2021). Lin and Zhang (2023) operationalized this architecture family in the LibMTL PyTorch library, which unifies 13 weighting strategies and 8 sharing architectures; their NYUv2 benchmarks showed that exact gradient-balancing methods demand per-task gradients over all shared parameters and require representation-level approximations when parameter counts grow, and their library initially lacked natural language processing backbones.

Two methodological studies define the statistical standards that the proposed benchmark adopts. Reimers and Gurevych (2017) retrained published sequence-tagging systems across dozens of random seeds and showed that single reported scores are statistically misleading: score variation across random seeds reached 1.01 F1 on CoNLL-2003 NER and 8.23 F1 on ACE 2005, and development-set selection correlated only weakly with test performance (Spearman ρ = 0.229). Demšar (2006) demonstrated that classifier comparisons across multiple datasets violate the normality and sphericity assumptions of parametric tests and recommended the Friedman test with the Iman-Davenport correction followed by post-hoc Nemenyi procedures as the default non-parametric protocol. These two studies jointly motivate the proposed design decisions of multi-seed evaluation under Leave-One-Event-Out (LOEO) cross-validation and two-tier non-parametric significance testing.

## **2.2 Multi-Task Optimization Dynamics: Balancing Conflicting Task Gradients**

### **2.2.1 Gradient Surgery and Projection Techniques**

Sener and Koltun (2018) reframed multi-task learning as multi-objective optimization and adapted the Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) with a Frank-Wolfe line search to locate Pareto-stationary updates. Recognizing that exact MGDA scales linearly with task count because the algorithm requires separate backward passes, the authors proposed MGDA-UB, which solves the min-norm problem over representation gradients in a single backward pass and accelerated training by a factor of 25 on the 40-task CelebA benchmark. Yu et al. (2020) identified the "tragic triad" of high curvature, disparate gradient magnitudes, and conflicting directions, and proposed Projecting Conflicting Gradients (PCGrad), which projects each task gradient onto the normal plane of any conflicting gradient with negative cosine similarity. PCGrad raised Multi-task CIFAR-100 accuracy to 71.0 percent (versus 67.7 percent for independent training) and solved all ten Meta-World MT10 manipulation tasks with multi-task Soft Actor-Critic where the vanilla joint agent failed half of the tasks; ablations showed that altering both gradient directions and magnitudes is necessary. The authors acknowledged two structural costs: convergence can stall when cosine similarity approaches −1, and computing per-task gradients multiplies backward passes linearly with task count.

Liu et al. (2021a) strengthened the convergence guarantees with Conflict-Averse Gradient Descent (CAGrad), which maximizes the worst-case local task improvement within a trust region around the average gradient and provably converges to the average-loss optimum when the hyperparameter c < 1. CAGrad halved the average performance drop on CityScapes (11.64 percent versus 22.60 percent for shared MTAN under equal weighting) and matched or surpassed PCGrad across NYUv2 and Meta-World, with a task-sampled variant delivering 2x to 5x speedups. Liu et al. (2021b) approached fairness directly with Impartial MTL (IMTL): IMTL-G computes closed-form scaling factors so that the aggregated gradient makes equal projections onto every raw task gradient, and IMTL-L rebalances loss scales through learnable parameters without distributional assumptions, while remaining invariant to manual loss rescaling and computable through a single backward pass over shared representations. Navon et al. (2022) grounded gradient aggregation in cooperative bargaining theory, deriving Nash-MTL as the unique scale-invariant update satisfying Pareto optimality, symmetry, and independence of irrelevant alternatives. Nash-MTL achieved the best overall trade-off on QM9 molecular regression (mean rank 2.5 versus 6.8 for linear scalarization, whose Δm reached 177.6 percent) and improved NYUv2 (Δm of −4.04 percent versus +0.20 percent for CAGrad), but each update requires solving a K-dimensional convex problem, which the authors mitigated by refreshing the bargaining solution only every 50 to 100 steps at a modest performance cost.

Recent methods rederive balancing from structural analyses of gradients or kernels. Senushkin et al. (2023) proved that the condition number κ(𝐆) of the task-gradient matrix jointly measures conflict and dominance, and proposed Aligned-MTL, which equalizes singular values through orthogonal Procrustes alignment and attains Δm of −8.40 percent on NYUv2 (MTAN) while requiring K backward passes in its exact form. Zhou et al. (2025) demonstrated empirically that gradient imbalance, rather than angular conflict, is the more fundamental obstacle, because balanced gradients converge despite conflicts whereas imbalanced gradients stall; their IMGrad method imposed projected-norm constraints and reached Δm of −4.57 percent on NYUv2 and a 0.93 success rate on Meta-World MT10. Qin et al. (2025) derived task weights from the eigenvalues of an extended Neural Tangent Kernel matrix (NTKMTL) to equalize convergence speeds, reporting a mean rank of 4.33 with Δm of −6.99 percent on NYUv2 and a shared-representation variant that runs at near-scalarization speed. Shi et al. (2023) shifted the intervention point from gradients to architecture with Recon, which identifies the shared layers with the highest pairwise conflict frequency during an initial search phase, converts those layers into task-specific parameters, and retrains; Recon reduced severe conflicts by 67 to 79 percent while duplicating only 0.26 to 12.77 percent of parameters, but the two-phase workflow requires a full retraining cycle and conflict scoring scales quadratically with task count.

### **2.2.2 Dynamic Loss-Weighting Methods**

An alternative algorithmic family adapts scalar loss weights without modifying gradient directions. Kendall et al. (2018) derived homoscedastic uncertainty weighting from a probabilistic likelihood in which each task receives a learnable variance parameter, producing the loss term ¹⁄₂σₖ² 𝓛ₖ + log σₖ. On CityScapes, uniform weighting degraded semantic segmentation to 50.1 percent IoU (below the 59.4 percent single-task level), whereas learned uncertainty weighting recovered 63.4 percent IoU with a final weight ratio of 43:1:0.16 across segmentation, depth, and instance tasks, and it outperformed grid-searched static weights (62.8 percent IoU) because the weights adapt during training. Chen et al. (2018) proposed GradNorm, which adjusts loss weights so that gradient norms at the final shared layer track relative inverse training rates; a single GradNorm run matched or surpassed the results of a 100-run weight grid search on NYUv2 (depth RMS error of 0.925 m at α = 1.5) at approximately 5 percent computational overhead. Liu et al. (2019a) introduced Dynamic Weight Average (DWA) within the Multi-Task Attention Network (MTAN), which balances tasks from prior-epoch loss descent rates alone, requires no additional backward passes, and made training robust to the choice of weighting scheme because attention-based feature selection implicitly balances tasks. Liu et al. (2023) subsequently proposed FAMO, which equalizes task log-loss improvement rates through a first-order update on weighting logits while amortizing all gradient computations into a single backward pass, achieving 𝒪(1) time and space per iteration. FAMO outperformed linear scalarization on NYUv2 (Δm of −4.10 percent versus +5.59 percent) and CelebA (40 tasks) and trained 25 times faster than Nash-MTL on a two-task synthetic benchmark, while the authors acknowledged sensitivity to the regularization decay hyperparameter γ and reliance on temporal loss differences as directional-derivative estimates.

### **2.2.3 Empirical Skepticism: Scalarization Baselines and Optimizer Confounds**

A converging body of evidence questions whether specialized MTO algorithms justify their costs when baselines are tuned properly. Kurin et al. (2022) benchmarked unitary (equal-weight) scalarization against MGDA, GradNorm, PCGrad, CAGrad, and IMTL across vision, reinforcement learning, and machine translation benchmarks under standardized hyperparameter budgets, and found that well-tuned equal weighting with Adam matched or exceeded every specialized method, while the specialized algorithms ran 2x to 35x slower because of their multiple backward passes; the authors interpreted many balancing schemes as implicit regularizers rather than superior optimizers. Xin et al. (2022) reached congruent conclusions on large-scale multilingual machine translation and vision: dynamic task weights assigned by MTO algorithms remained nearly constant during training, per-task gradient computation reduced training throughput from 11.52 to 4.81 steps per second, reallocating that budget to model depth strictly improved the Pareto frontier, and learning-rate underspecification produced six to seven times more score variance than seed variation, creating illusions of algorithmic superiority in undertuned comparisons.

Elich et al. (2024) identified the standard optimizer itself as the decisive confound. Across CityScapes, NYUv2, and CelebA, Adam outperformed SGD with momentum for every architecture and weighting scheme, and the authors proved that Adam is partially loss-scale invariant for task-specific heads because loss scaling factors cancel between the first- and second-moment updates, while optimal uncertainty weighting is fully scale invariant. Under Adam, plain Equal Weighting (EW) achieved Pareto-optimal performance in three of four benchmark configurations and was not dominated by any specialized MTO method trained with SGD. Most consequentially for diagnostic methodology, Elich et al. (2024) measured gradient cosine similarities between samples within a single task and found that intra-task, inter-sample conflicts occur as frequently as cross-task conflicts, while gradient magnitude disparities, not angular misalignment, constitute the primary optimization challenge under adaptive optimization. Task-selection studies reinforce this caution: Standley et al. (2020) showed that joint-training task affinity is uncorrelated with transfer affinity (Pearson ρ = -0.12) and that all-in-one networks are often suboptimal under inference time budgets, while Kim et al. (2023) executed approximately 25,000 pairwise transfer experiments over 22 NLP tasks and found that transfer is predominantly non-commutative and that curated small task mixtures outperform indiscriminate multi-task training. Collectively, these results indicate that claims of balancing gains must be established under optimizer-controlled, multi-seed, budget-matched conditions on the target domain, which is precisely the condition under which the proposed Taglish triage benchmark operates.

## **2.3 Code-Switched Text Processing and Low-Resource Philippine NLP**

### **2.3.1 Computational Challenges of Code-Switched Language**

Systematic surveys converge on structural obstacles that confront code-switched natural language processing. Doğruöz et al. (2021) argued that computational work largely ignores grammatical and sociolinguistic theories of switching, that multilingual pretrained models underperform on code-switched inputs because pretraining corpora lack naturalistic mixed language, and that benchmarks cover predominantly Spanish-English and Hindi-English pairs. Winata et al. (2023) reviewed more than 400 publications across three decades and documented severe resource concentration around English-paired bilingual data, dominance of language identification and sentiment tasks, and dataset attrition, with over 60 percent of surveyed corpora no longer publicly accessible. Sheth et al. (2026) synthesized 327 studies spanning the large language model era and reported that large language models frequently underperform smaller fine-tuned task-specific models on code-switched inputs, that over 72 percent of speech and 92 percent of social media code-switching datasets involve English-centric pairs, and that standard generation metrics correlate poorly with human judgments on mixed-language output. Southeast Asian and Austronesian contact varieties, including Tagalog-English code-switching (Taglish), remain critically underrepresented in all three surveys.

Subword tokenization quantifies a concrete computational penalty for underrepresented contact varieties. Ahia et al. (2023) measured token fertility across commercial tokenizers and showed that identical semantic content in low-resource languages requires 2x to 7x more subword tokens than English, inflating per-token API costs and inference latency. Petrov et al. (2023) extended the analysis across open multilingual tokenizers and documented sequence-length inflation of up to 15x for non-Latin scripts, which prematurely exhausts context windows and increases latency. Nag et al. (2024) formulated a Relative Performance to Cost Ratio over 15 Indian languages and demonstrated that tokenization over-fragmentation is most severe for typologically distant languages, while client-side preprocessing recovered up to approximately 90 percent of API cost. For a Taglish triage pipeline, these findings imply that subword over-fragmentation dilutes token-level embeddings for NER and increases optimization variance precisely where supervision is densest, motivating multilingual encoders whose vocabularies cover Tagalog-English mixing.

### **2.3.2 Multi-Task Learning on Code-Switched Text**

Small but growing literature tests multi-task architectures on code-switched text, and the findings remain mixed. Khanuja et al. (2020) released GLUECoS, a six-task benchmark for English-Hindi and English-Spanish code-switching, and showed that mBERT fine-tuned on a synthetic-then-real code-switched curriculum raised NER F1 to 78.21 (versus 74.96 for vanilla mBERT), while natural language inference remained near chance; the authors explicitly identified a unified multi-task learning setup for cross-task transfer as unexecuted future work. Sheth et al. (2025) constructed COMI-LINGUA, 125,615 expert-annotated Hindi-English instances with Fleiss' kappa of at least 0.817 across five tasks, and showed that parameter-efficient fine-tuning of 7-8B models reached up to 95.25 NER F1, exceeding zero-shot prompting of commercial large language models. Most directly relevant to the proposed research, Adouane and Bernardy (2020) conducted controlled pairwise MTL experiments on low-resource, noisy, code-switched Algerian user-generated text spanning code-switch detection, NER, spelling normalization, and sentiment analysis. The results demonstrated that multi-task benefits are conditional rather than universal: pairing NER with sentiment analysis induced negative transfer that collapsed NER macro F1 from 49.68 to 34.60, whereas pairing NER with code-switch detection produced mutual gains that raised detection macro F1 from 64.54 to 71.29. The authors further documented that gains were frequently transient, concentrated in the first epochs before noise and sparsity degraded joint training, and they identified principled loss balancing as the missing mechanism. This evidence establishes both the promise and the risk of the proposed three-task Taglish bundle and supplies the strongest empirical motivation for benchmarking explicit MTO algorithms rather than assuming automatic joint-training benefits.

### **2.3.3 Philippine Language Resources and Local Disaster Response Studies**

Philippine NLP has matured from vocabulary-scale models to benchmark infrastructure, but along a trajectory that bypasses disaster-domain multi-tasking. Cruz and Cheng (2022) pretrained RoBERTa Base (110M) and Large (330M) models on the TLUnified corpus and improved test accuracy over prior Filipino BERT baselines by an average of 4.47 points (72.67 percent on election hate speech detection versus 68.60 percent), while observing only marginal gains from scaling Base to Large (+0.09 to +0.90 points) because of pretraining corpus size and delimiting evaluation to classification without sequence labeling. Miranda (2023) addressed sequence labeling with TLUNIFIED-NER, a 208,247-token news corpus annotated for Person, Organization, and Location spans with inter-annotator Cohen's kappa of 0.81 to 0.83 and reference F1 of 0.89; fine-tuned supervised models consistently outperformed zero-shot prompting of commercial and open large language models, and the corpus covers formal news text rather than informal code-switched social media. Montalan et al. (2025) introduced Batayan, an eight-task Filipino and Taglish benchmark of 3,800 natively validated test instances, showing that Southeast Asian instruction-tuned models such as Gemma-SEA-LION-v3-9B reached an average macro F1 of 79.23 on natural language understanding tasks; evaluation is task-isolated and excludes the disaster domain. Herrera et al. (2022) contributed TweetTaglish, 21,150 code-switched tweets with token- and tweet-level language distribution annotations (average composition of 55.2 percent Tagalog, 19.4 percent English, and 24.5 percent other tokens), validated at instance-level kappa of 0.70, with proof-of-concept regressors reaching R² of 0.909 for English proportion prediction; the resource contains no triage annotations. Africa et al. (2025) improved zero-shot NER transfer to Tagalog and Cebuano by 2 to 6 micro F1 points through meta-pretraining of small causal decoders, confirming both the feasibility of cross-lingual transfer for Philippine languages and the concentration of gains on person entities. On the encoder side, Warner et al. (2025) demonstrated with ModernBERT that hardware-aware depth, full-sequence unpadding, and alternating local-global attention deliver 2.65x to 3.0x throughput gains at long contexts, informing efficient encoder selection for the SEA-LION ModernBERT candidate backbone, while the authors delimited the model family to English text and code.

Local disaster-focused studies address single tasks on single events with non-Transformer models. Imperial et al. (2019) classified sentiment in 3,900 annotated Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan) tweets with standard and bidirectional recurrent networks, reaching 81.79 percent accuracy for three-class and 87.69 percent accuracy for binary classification, with false negatives accounting for 12 to 14 percent of errors. Ermino et al. (2022) trained a lightweight feedforward artificial neural network on 4,020 Typhoon Ulysses (Vamco) tweets for binary urgency evaluation, reporting 99.17 percent validation accuracy and an F1 of 0.9882 on a 100-tweet confusion-matrix set, while acknowledging a fixed 8,000-word vocabulary and a single-event training distribution as limitations. At the cross-lingual frontier, Kayi et al. (2020) transferred urgency classification from English to Sinhala and Odia, reaching 75.7 macro F1 on English with RoBERTa-large but only 63.5 and 62.6 zero-shot macro F1 on the target languages, with annotators conflating general status updates with actionable distress calls. Ray Chowdhury et al. (2020) aggregated 130,000 crisis tweets and applied Manifold Mixup to multilingual BERT, raising out-of-event macro F1 to 0.8414-0.8688 and achieving 78.43 percent macro F1 on the Philippines Flood test event, with caution-and-advice remaining the weakest class at 62.33 F1. Across this body of work, no study combines token-level entity extraction, intent categorization, and urgency prioritization on code-switched Philippine disaster text under cross-event evaluation.

## **2.5 Synthesis of Related Literature and Studies**

Three established trends emerge across the reviewed literature. First, crisis informatics has converged on Transformer encoders as the state-of-the-art backbone for humanitarian message processing, with monolingual RoBERTa-class models leading English benchmarks (weighted F1 of 0.872 on CrisisBench and 0.781 on HumAID) and DistilBERT-class variants demonstrating comparable accuracy at lower parameter counts for deployment-oriented settings (Alam et al., 2021a, 2021b). Second, joint multi-task modeling of token-level and sequence-level tasks is an accepted methodology that outperforms independent pipelines when tasks are compatible, as demonstrated in intent detection and slot filling (Weld et al., 2022) and in disaster categorization with shared encoders (Wang et al., 2021), with hard parameter sharing providing effective 𝒪(1) inference cost relative to task count (Caruana, 1997; Ruder, 2017; Zhang & Yang, 2021). Third, the MTO literature has produced two mature algorithmic families, dynamic loss weighting (Kendall et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2023) and gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a, 2021b; Navon et al., 2022; Senushkin et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025), evaluated with increasing methodological rigor under multi-seed, non-parametric protocols (Demšar, 2006; Reimers & Gurevych, 2017). For code-switched and low-resource text, the field additionally agrees that multilingual encoders, careful annotation validation, and tokenization-aware design are prerequisites for usable systems (Doğruöz et al., 2021; Khanuja et al., 2020; Ahia et al., 2023; Petrov et al., 2023).

Four unresolved bottlenecks persist at the intersection of these trends. First, the central empirical dispute in MTO remains unresolved: tuned static scalarization with adaptive optimizers matches or exceeds specialized balancing algorithms on vision and translation benchmarks (Elich et al., 2024; Kurin et al., 2022; Xin et al., 2022), yet every audit study operated on well-resourced, monolingual, or vision data, so the question of whether dynamic balancing earns its 2x to 35x runtime overhead on low-resource, code-switched crisis text remains unanswered. Second, the disaster MTL studies closest to the proposed problem stop short of optimization control: Wang et al. (2021) applied fixed weighting without gradient diagnostics or efficiency measurements, and Ali et al. (2026) reported token-versus-sequence gradient asymmetry that degraded classification but measured neither gradient norms nor cosine similarities directly, and neither study reported inference latency, GPU memory, or cross-event generalization. Third, no existing corpus combines token-level entity spans, intent categories, and urgency tiers on code-switched Taglish disaster text: Philippine resources are task-isolated, formal-domain, or language-ID-oriented (Cruz & Cheng, 2022; Miranda, 2023; Montalan et al., 2025; Herrera et al., 2022), local disaster studies address single tasks on single events with non-Transformer models (Imperial et al., 2019; Ermino et al., 2022), and prior code-switched MTL evidence warns that unconditional joint training can collapse minority task performance (Adouane & Bernardy, 2020). Fourth, diagnostic confounding remains unaddressed in applied settings: Elich et al. (2024) showed that intra-task, inter-sample gradient conflicts rival cross-task conflicts, but the decoupling procedure exists only in computer vision, and no crisis informatics study separates genuine cross-task interference from mini-batch variance when justifying balancing overhead.

The proposed study bridges these gaps through four concrete contributions mapped directly onto the bottlenecks. To resolve the unresolved MTO dispute on the target domain, the study benchmarks seven candidate algorithms spanning both algorithmic families, dynamic loss weighting (Uncertainty Weighting, GradNorm, FAMO) and gradient surgery (PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL-G, Nash-MTL), against isolated Single-Task Learning, Uniform Equal Weighting, and tuned Static Linear Scalarization under an equal hyperparameter search budget, AdamW optimization (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), and a 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out protocol across three seeds, directly extending the audit conditions of Elich et al. (2024) and Kurin et al. (2022) to code-switched crisis text. To supply the missing optimization evidence in disaster MTL, the framework integrates routines for logging gradients in PyTorch that record per-task gradient norms, norm ratios, and pairwise cosine similarities on shared encoder layers, paired with an intra-task baseline derived from half-batch gradients that operationalizes the decoupling principle of Elich et al. (2024) for crisis triage and enables Spearman correlation analysis between transfer gains Δm and measured conflict dynamics. To close the dataset void, the study constructs the Multi-Task Corpus of Taglish Disaster Tweets, providing concurrent, expert-adjudicated annotations for token-level entity spans, intent categories, and urgency tiers across four historical Philippine typhoons, thereby uniting the annotation depth of HumAID-NER (Ali et al., 2026) with the code-switched linguistic coverage of TweetTaglish (Herrera et al., 2022). Finally, to ground deployment relevance, the benchmark records single-instance latency, batched throughput, and peak GPU and host memory under FP16 execution on an 8GB workstation, converting the deferred efficiency questions of Wang et al. (2021) into measured quantities, and it integrates the resulting model into a prototype web application for disaster triage as the empirical demonstration vehicle required by Design Science Research evaluation. Through these four bridges, the study converts an unresolved optimization question, an undiagnosed gradient asymmetry, a missing multilingual corpus, and an unmeasured deployment cost into a single reproducible empirical benchmark for multi-task crisis triage on Taglish disaster tweets.
