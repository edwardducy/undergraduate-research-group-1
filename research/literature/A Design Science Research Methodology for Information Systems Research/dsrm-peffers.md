# A Design Science Research Methodology for Information Systems Research

Ken Peffers, Tuure Tuunanen, Marcus A. Rothenberger, and Samir Chatterjee  
*Journal of Management Information Systems* (JMIS), Vol. 24, No. 3, pp. 45–77, 2007  
DOI: 10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302

---

## Abstract

Design science research (DSR) is of increasing importance in information systems (IS), but the lack of a standard methodology has created uncertainty about how to conduct and evaluate such research. This paper develops and evaluates a design science research methodology (DSRM) process model that provides a mental model, nominal process, and presentation template for conducting DSR in IS. The DSRM process includes six steps: problem identification and motivation, definition of the objectives for a solution, design and development, demonstration, evaluation, and communication. The methodology is evaluated through four case studies illustrating different research entry points.

---

## Core Process Model (The 6 DSRM Activities)

1. **Activity 1: Problem Identification and Motivation** - Define the specific research problem and justify the value of a solution. Atomize the problem conceptually to capture its complexity and motivate researchers and audience.
2. **Activity 2: Define Objectives of a Solution** - Infer the objectives of a solution from the problem definition and knowledge of what is possible and feasible.
3. **Activity 3: Design and Development** - Create the artifact (constructs, models, methods, or instantiations). Determine the artifact desired functionality and architecture.
4. **Activity 4: Demonstration** - Demonstrate the use of the artifact to solve one or more instances of the problem via experimentation, simulation, case study, or proof.
5. **Activity 5: Evaluation** - Observe and measure how well the artifact supports a solution to the problem, comparing solution objectives to observed empirical results.
6. **Activity 6: Communication** - Disseminate the problem, artifact utility, design rigor, and effectiveness to researchers and practicing professionals.
