| **A Survey of Code-switching: Linguistic and Social Perspectives for Language Technologies**<br><br>*A. Seza Doğruöz, Sunayana Sitaram, Barbara E. Code-switching is strongly driven by pragmatic, cognitive, and socio-indexical functions (such as expressing in-group solidarity, politeness, conversational topic, and affective stance), which leave predictable structural traces that are ignored in conventional NLP pipelines.; Massive pretrained multilingual language models (e.g., mBERT, XLM-R) perform substantially worse on code-switched tasks than monolingual settings because they are trained on web-scraped monolingual corpora (Wikipedia, Common Crawl) lacking natural spoken and informal code-switching varieties.; Standardized code-switching benchmarks (e.g., GLUE-CoS, LINCE) are limited to a narrow set of language pairs (primarily Spanish-English and Hindi-English) and social media domains (Twitter), with state-of-the-art models performing near chance levels on complex reasoning tasks like Natural Language Inference (NLI).; synthetic code-switched generation methods rely on rigid grammatical frameworks (e.g., MLF) that fail to generate natural utterances; and existing research lacks systematic collection and modeling of negative evidence where social context inhibits switching. |
