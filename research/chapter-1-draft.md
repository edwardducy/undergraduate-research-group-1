# Chapter 1: The Problem and Its Background

## 4. Statement of the Problem

### 4.1 General Problem Statement

<!-- vale off -->
Disaster triage systems must process social media posts across three concurrent natural language processing tasks, including token-level Named Entity Recognition (NER), sequence-level intent classification, and sequence-level urgency classification. Deploying isolated Single-Task Learning (STL) models increases computational latency and concurrent memory usage linearly ($\mathcal{O}(K)$ for $K$ tasks) (Caruana, 1997, Ruder, 2017). In contrast, a shared Transformer encoder extracts features for the same tweet in a single forward pass ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ relative to task count $K$). However, joint multi-task training couples token-level and sequence-level loss functions. This parameter coupling causes differences in gradient magnitudes and conflicting gradient directions ($\cos(\mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j) < 0$), which degrades accuracy in shared encoder layers through negative transfer (Yu et al., 2020, Liu et al., 2021). Specialized Multi-Task Optimization (MTO) algorithms, spanning dynamic loss weighting methods and gradient surgery techniques, aim to balance conflicting gradients. Prior literature reports conflicting findings on whether dynamic balancing algorithms outperform tuned linear scalarization or simple equal weighting (Xin et al., 2022, Kurin et al., 2022, Elich et al., 2024). Furthermore, intra-task sample diversity also produces opposing gradient directions, which can confound cross-task gradient conflict measurements (Elich et al., 2024). In addition, existing disaster informatics benchmarks lack unified Taglish corpora containing concurrent annotations for token-level entities, message intent, and urgency. This study therefore constructs a Multi-Task Taglish Disaster Tweet Corpus ($N \approx 6,000\text{--}10,000$ tweets across four Philippine disaster events) partitioned under a Leave-One-Event-Out (LOEO) cross-validation protocol, and benchmarks candidate Multi-Task Optimization algorithms against Uniform Equal Weighting and Single-Task Learning baselines. The experimental framework integrates gradient logging routines in PyTorch to record per-task gradient norms and pairwise cosine similarities alongside an Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline on a dedicated local CUDA GPU workstation to measure whether parameter sharing causes negative transfer and whether balancing algorithms restore single-task baseline accuracy.

### 4.2 Specific Problem Statements

Specifically, this study addresses the following research questions aligned with the Design Science Research (DSR) framework.

1. What are the baseline task F1 scores (Intent Classification Macro F1, Urgency Classification Macro F1, and Named Entity Recognition Span F1), gradient norms, gradient norm ratios, inference latency ($t_{\text{exec}}$), and concurrent memory footprints ($M_{\text{RAM}}$) of isolated Single-Task Learning baselines compared to a shared multilingual Transformer encoder trained under Uniform Equal Weighting (EW) on the constructed Leave-One-Event-Out Taglish disaster tweets?
2. How can the multi-task multilingual Transformer encoder, candidate Multi-Task Optimization (MTO) algorithms, and gradient diagnostic logging routines be implemented in PyTorch, and deployed into an Operational Disaster Triage Web Application featuring a FastAPI asynchronous inference backend and an interactive responder map dashboard?
3. How do the benchmarked Multi-Task Optimization algorithms compare to Static Linear Scalarization (LS) and Single-Task Learning baselines across 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out disaster splits in task F1 scores, relative multi-task transfer ($\Delta_m$), and training runtime?
4. Do performance differences among the Multi-Task Optimization algorithms achieve statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) across disaster event splits and random seeds under non-parametric testing, and do these differences show Spearman rank correlation with measured gradient cosine similarities and gradient norm ratios relative to the Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline?

## 6. Theoretical Framework

<!-- vale off -->
This study is grounded in three recognized scientific paradigms, including Design Science Research Methodology, Computational Complexity Theory in Representation Sharing, and Multi-Objective Optimization with Gradient Dynamics Principles.

### 6.1 Design Science Research Methodology

This study adopts the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) established by Peffers et al. (2007) and the artifact evaluation principles formulated by Hevner et al. (2004). Unlike behavioral sciences that focus on observing and predicting phenomena, design science in computer science produces new scientific knowledge through the systematic design, construction, and empirical evaluation of innovative computational artifacts. 

Hevner et al. (2004) establish that valid design science research requires rigorous evaluation of artifact utility, problem relevance, and verifiable research contributions. Peffers et al. (2007) formulated this inquiry as an iterative six-activity lifecycle moving from problem identification to artifact design, demonstration, and evaluation. To operationalize this methodology for computer science undergraduate research, the CEU CSIT research guidelines adapt DSRM into four sequential phases, comprising Baseline Analysis (Phase 1), Design and Artifact Creation (Phase 2), Empirical Benchmarking (Phase 3), and Comparative Validation (Phase 4). This theoretical foundation ensures that the software triage application operates as a functional vehicle to empirically evaluate the primary algorithmic contribution.

### 6.2 Computational Complexity Theory in Representation Sharing

This study applies Computational Complexity Theory alongside the inductive bias principles formulated by Caruana (1997) and Baxter (2000) to formalize computational time and space trade-offs in neural natural language processing.

In single-task learning architectures, deploying $K$ independent models to process $K$ natural language processing tasks requires $K$ separate forward passes for each input tweet. The computational inference latency scales linearly with task count as $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot T_{\text{backbone}})$. Similarly, maintaining $K$ concurrent models in memory requires $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot |\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{backbone}}|)$ parameter storage.

In contrast, hard parameter sharing amortizes intermediate feature representation extraction into a single shared forward pass. Feature extraction operates in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time relative to task count $K$, while parameter memory allocation is restricted to $\mathcal{O}(|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{backbone}}| + \sum_{k=1}^K |\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{head},k}|)$. Because task classification heads contain less than one percent of total network parameters, hard parameter sharing achieves asymptotic computational savings in both execution time and memory footprint during real-time disaster triage. Furthermore, learning shared intermediate representations across related tasks restricts the search space to hypothesis subsets that explain multiple tasks simultaneously (Baxter, 2000), improving generalization on low-resource Taglish text.

### 6.3 Multi-Objective Optimization and Gradient Dynamics Principles

This study is anchored in Multi-Objective Optimization (Sener and Koltun, 2018) and Gradient Dynamics Principles (Yu et al., 2020, Liu et al., 2021, Chen et al., 2018, Kendall et al., 2018, Xin et al., 2022, Elich et al., 2024). In a hard-parameter-sharing neural network with shared encoder parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}$ and task-specific heads $\boldsymbol{\theta}_k$, joint training represents a multi-objective optimization problem.

$$\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\theta}_K} \left( \mathcal{L}_1(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_1), \mathcal{L}_2(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_2), \dots, \mathcal{L}_K(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_K) \right)$$

Multi-objective optimization theory establishes that multi-task learning seeks a Pareto-optimal parameter configuration where no individual task loss can be decreased without increasing the loss of another task. When updating shared parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}$ through gradient descent with learning rate $\eta$, applying a first-order Taylor expansion to the loss of task $j$ under a parameter step along task $i$ yields a direct mathematical relationship.

$$\mathcal{L}_j(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}} - \eta \mathbf{g}_i) \approx \mathcal{L}_j(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}) - \eta \langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle$$

This formulation reveals two primary optimization failure modes in shared parameter spaces.

- **Gradient Magnitude Disparity.** When loss functions operate across disparate granularities, gradient magnitudes diverge substantially ($\|\mathbf{g}_i\|_2 \gg \|\mathbf{g}_j\|_2$). The dominant task gradient controls parameter trajectory, causing slower-learning tasks to suffer from under-optimization (Chen et al., 2018).
- **Gradient Directional Conflict.** When the inner product between task gradients is negative ($\langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle < 0$, or cosine similarity $\cos(\mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j) < 0$), the term $-\eta \langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle$ becomes positive. This condition mathematically guarantees that parameter updates along task $i$ increase the loss on task $j$, inducing negative transfer across shared layers (Yu et al., 2020).

Specialized Multi-Task Optimization (MTO) algorithms address these challenges by dynamically rescaling loss weights or projecting conflicting gradient vectors onto orthogonal hyperplanes. Furthermore, this study incorporates the empirical gradient theory of Elich et al. (2024), which demonstrated that stochastic mini-batch sample diversity also produces opposing gradient directions within a single task. The theoretical framework integrates an Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline to decouple stochastic sample variance from genuine cross-task gradient conflict, establishing a controlled foundation for non-parametric statistical hypothesis testing (Demšar, 2006).

### 7.1 Input

The input stage establishes the empirical data, network architectures, optimization algorithms, and testbed hardware constraints. The linguistic data consists of the curated Multi-Task Taglish Disaster Tweet Corpus ($N \approx 6,000\text{--}10,000$ tweets) collected across four Philippine disaster events (Typhoon Haiyan, Typhoon Vamco, Typhoon Rai, and Typhoon Paeng), annotated for Named Entity Recognition (NER) location and infrastructure spans in BIO format, message intent categories, and urgency priority levels. A 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out partitioning protocol reserves distinct disaster events exclusively for evaluation. The architectural input defines a shared multilingual Transformer encoder backbone (such as XLM-RoBERTa Base with $\approx 270\text{M}$ parameters or mBERT Base with $\approx 110\text{M}$ parameters) with hard parameter sharing across intermediate representation layers, connected to specialized sequence labeling and sequence classification heads, alongside isolated Single-Task Learning baselines. The algorithmic input encompasses candidate Multi-Task Optimization algorithms across dynamic loss weighting and gradient surgery families, benchmarked against Uniform Equal Weighting and Static Linear Scalarization sweeps under fixed hyperparameter constraints on a dedicated local Linux workstation equipped with an NVIDIA CUDA GPU ($\ge 8\text{GB}$ VRAM), multi-core CPU, and 32GB RAM.

### 7.2 Process

The process stage executes the computational pipeline across data engineering, multi-task model training, gradient diagnostic tracking, empirical benchmarking, and software artifact deployment. The data engineering module performs text normalization, code-switched tokenization, subword span alignment, and 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out cross-validation batch compilation. The training module executes joint multi-task optimization in PyTorch, computing heterogeneous loss functions and updating shared encoder weights through candidate balancing algorithms. The gradient diagnostic logging routines record per-task gradient norms, gradient norm ratios, and pairwise cosine similarities on flattened shared parameters at each optimization step, comparing these values against an Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline to isolate cross-task conflicts from stochastic sample variance. The benchmarking module profiles single-pass inference latency in milliseconds ($t_{\text{exec}}$), peak concurrent memory in megabytes ($M_{\text{RAM}}$), throughput in tweets per second, task F1 scores, relative multi-task transfer ($\Delta_m$), non-parametric hypothesis testing ($p < 0.05$), and Spearman rank correlation. Finally, the software engineering module deploys the fine-tuned model into a FastAPI asynchronous inference backend and builds an Interactive Responder Map Dashboard.

### 7.3 Output

The output stage produces four primary deliverables across algorithmic, empirical, statistical, and software domains. The algorithmic output provides a fine-tuned multilingual Transformer model that executes Named Entity Recognition, intent classification, and urgency scoring concurrently in a single forward pass ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ relative to task count $K$). The empirical output delivers validated benchmark tables and gradient diagnostic datasets across all 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out disaster splits. The statistical output yields non-parametric hypothesis test proofs ($p < 0.05$) and Spearman correlation coefficients demonstrating whether Multi-Task Optimization algorithms resolve negative transfer and restore single-task predictive accuracy. The software output delivers the Operational Disaster Triage Web Application that serves real-time multi-task predictions and displays interactive incident maps for emergency response dispatchers.

## 8. Scope and Delimitations

### 8.1 Scope of the Study

<!-- vale off -->
This study focuses on constructing the Multi-Task Taglish Disaster Tweet Corpus, benchmarking Multi-Task Optimization (MTO) algorithms in a shared multilingual Transformer encoder, and deploying the trained model into an Operational Disaster Triage Web Application. The investigation evaluates three concurrent natural language processing tasks, including token-level Named Entity Recognition (NER) for critical location and infrastructure spans, sequence-level intent classification, and sequence-level urgency classification. The neural network architecture uses a shared multilingual Transformer encoder (encoder-only backbones such as XLM-RoBERTa Base with $\approx 270\text{M}$ parameters and multilingual BERT Base with $\approx 110\text{M}$ parameters) with hard parameter sharing across intermediate representation layers, connected to task-specific sequence classification and sequence labeling heads. The optimization benchmark evaluates candidate balancing algorithms across two algorithmic families, spanning dynamic loss weighting methods (Uncertainty Weighting, GradNorm, and FAMO) and gradient surgery techniques (PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL, and Nash-MTL), benchmarked against Uniform Equal Weighting ($w_k = 1/K$), Static Linear Scalarization sweeps, and isolated Single-Task Learning baselines within PyTorch.

Because no public multi-task dataset exists for Taglish disaster triage, this study constructs and manually validates the Multi-Task Taglish Disaster Tweet Corpus ($N \approx 6,000\text{--}10,000$ tweets) collected across four historical Philippine disaster events: Typhoon Haiyan (2013), Typhoon Vamco (2020), Typhoon Rai (2021), and Typhoon Paeng (2022). The experimental methodology partitions this corpus under a 4-fold Leave-One-Event-Out (LOEO) cross-validation protocol, reserving tweets from each specific disaster incident exclusively for out-of-distribution evaluation. The framework evaluates model performance through task-specific F1 metrics comprising Intent Classification Macro F1, Urgency Classification Macro F1, and NER Span F1, alongside the relative multi-task transfer metric $\Delta_m$ (defined as average percentage performance gain over Single-Task Learning baselines, following Maninis et al., 2019). The training framework integrates gradient diagnostic logging routines to record per-task gradient norms and pairwise gradient cosine similarities across flattened shared encoder parameters alongside an Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline computed on disjoint half-batches. The benchmark evaluates single-pass inference latency in milliseconds ($t_{\text{exec}}$), peak concurrent memory allocation in megabytes ($M_{\text{RAM}}$), throughput in tweets per second, and training execution time on a dedicated local testbed (Linux Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, dedicated NVIDIA CUDA GPU with $\ge 8\text{GB}$ VRAM, multi-core CPU, and 32GB RAM). Statistical significance is evaluated using the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired comparisons and the Friedman test across event splits ($p < 0.05$), with Spearman rank correlation applied to evaluate relationships between relative transfer ($\Delta_m$) and gradient conflict metrics.

The software artifact incorporates the trained multi-task model into an Operational Disaster Triage Web Application. This software comprises a FastAPI asynchronous inference backend and an Interactive Responder Map Dashboard. The backend receives text inputs, executes multi-task inference, and returns structured predictions. The dashboard visualizes extracted entity locations on a map, displays classified intent and urgency labels, and tracks incident workflow states from initial receipt to resolution.

### 8.2 Delimitations of the Study

This study limits linguistic analysis strictly to code-switched Taglish text within the disaster management domain. The research excludes other Philippine regional languages, such as Cebuano, Ilocano, and Hiligaynon, because Taglish serves as the primary language for online disaster communications in the Philippines, and constructing multi-task annotation corpora across multiple regional languages exceeds the scope of this study. The dataset construction is delimited to public crisis tweets collected across selected historical Philippine disaster events, excluding private messaging platforms and non-crisis social media threads.

The experimental scope restricts model architectures to hard-parameter-sharing encoder-only Transformer backbones. Consequently, this investigation does not evaluate soft parameter sharing, task-specific adapter modules, or generative Large Language Models that require extensive cloud GPU infrastructure.

The input pipeline processes textual data exclusively and does not process non-text inputs such as satellite imagery, audio recordings, or video broadcasts. The operational triage prototype consumes simulated event playback from the constructed disaster corpus and manual user submissions. Therefore, the implementation does not connect to live social media streaming scrapers to prevent external API rate-limiting constraints and ensure experimental reproducibility. The investigation is delimited to the three core operational triage tasks and does not evaluate secondary NLP tasks such as sentiment analysis, machine translation, or automated text summarization.

## 10. Definition of Terms

### 10.1 Operational Definitions

To establish technical precision across the study, the following terms are operationally defined according to their computational, mathematical, and algorithmic roles in the experimental framework and software system.

- **BIO Annotation Schema:** The token-level sequence labeling tag format that assigns Beginning (B-), Inside (I-), or Outside (O-) tags to identify contiguous multi-token location and infrastructure entity spans within Taglish crisis messages.
- **Computational Inference Latency ($t_{\text{exec}}$):** The elapsed execution duration in milliseconds required to execute a single forward pass and compute predictions across all three disaster triage tasks for an input tweet, measured using synchronized CUDA events on the dedicated testbed after discarding warm-up iterations:
  $$t_{\text{exec}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( t_{\text{end}, i} - t_{\text{start}, i} \right)$$
- **Dynamic Loss Weighting Methods:** The multi-task optimization algorithmic family that recomputes adaptive task loss coefficients $w_k^{(t)} > 0$ dynamically at each optimization step $t$ based on training statistics (such as homoscedastic uncertainty, inverse gradient norms, or loss decrease rates) to minimize $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}^{(t)} = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k^{(t)} \mathcal{L}_k$ without modifying gradient vector directions.
- **FastAPI Asynchronous Inference Backend:** The high-throughput Python web service component of the triage software artifact that ingests Taglish disaster tweet payloads asynchronously, executes single-pass inference through the fine-tuned multi-task Transformer model, and returns structured JSON predictions for entity spans, intent categories, and urgency levels.
- **Gradient Diagnostic Logging:** The PyTorch training routines and hooks that compute and record per-task gradient norms ($\|\mathbf{g}_t\|_2$), gradient norm ratios, and pairwise cosine similarities ($\cos(\mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j)$) on shared encoder parameters at every optimization step.
- **Gradient Directional Conflict:** The mathematical condition where the gradient vectors $\mathbf{g}_i = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_i$ and $\mathbf{g}_j = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_j$ with respect to shared encoder parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ produce a negative inner product ($\langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle < 0$) and cosine similarity ($\cos(\mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j) < 0$), mathematically guaranteeing that parameter updates along task $i$ degrade the empirical loss on task $j$.
- **Gradient Magnitude Disparity:** The scalar ratio $R_{\text{max/min}}^{(t)} = \frac{\max_{k} \|\mathbf{g}_k^{(t)}\|_2}{\min_{k} \|\mathbf{g}_k^{(t)}\|_2}$ computed at training step $t$ across shared encoder gradient vectors $\mathbf{g}_k^{(t)} = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_k^{(t)}$, where $L_2$ norms quantify the degree to which dominant task gradients overpower shared parameter updates.
- **Gradient Surgery Techniques:** The multi-task optimization algorithmic family (such as PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL, and Nash-MTL) that directly projects task gradient vectors $\mathbf{g}_k = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_k$ in Euclidean parameter space before backpropagation updates, replacing conflicting vector components ($\langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle < 0$) with orthogonal projections $\mathbf{g}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{g}_i - \frac{\langle \mathbf{g}_i, \mathbf{g}_j \rangle}{\|\mathbf{g}_j\|_2^2} \mathbf{g}_j$ to guarantee non-conflicting descent directions.
- **Hard-Parameter-Sharing Multilingual Transformer Encoder:** The deep neural network architecture where intermediate representation layers of an encoder-only multilingual Transformer backbone are shared across all tasks to extract contextual representations in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time relative to task count, from which task-specific prediction heads branch independently.
- **Inductive Bias in Representation Sharing:** The machine learning mechanism whereby optimizing shared encoder parameters across concurrent disaster triage tasks restricts the hypothesis space to representations that explain token-level entities, message intent, and urgency simultaneously, improving generalization on low-resource Taglish text.
- **Intent Classification Macro F1:** The unweighted arithmetic mean of class-specific F1 scores across all $|C_{\text{intent}}|$ humanitarian intent categories, calculated as:
  $$\text{Macro F1}_{\text{Intent}} = \frac{1}{|C_{\text{intent}}|} \sum_{c=1}^{|C_{\text{intent}}|} \frac{2 \cdot P_c \cdot R_c}{P_c + R_c}$$
  where $P_c$ and $R_c$ denote precision and recall for intent class $c$ predicted from the pooled sequence embedding $\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]}$.
- **Interactive Responder Map Dashboard:** The operational web frontend interface that plots geospatial pins corresponding to extracted NER location spans, visualizes classified intent and urgency tiers, and manages emergency dispatch workflow states from initial ingestion to resolution.
- **Intra-Task Half-Batch Gradient Baseline:** The diagnostic control metric computed between gradient vectors of two disjoint half-batches $\mathcal{B}_k^{(1)}$ and $\mathcal{B}_k^{(2)}$ from the same task:
  $$\cos_{\text{intra}, k} = \frac{\langle \mathbf{g}_k^{(1)}, \mathbf{g}_k^{(2)} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{g}_k^{(1)}\|_2 \|\mathbf{g}_k^{(2)}\|_2}$$
  establishing an empirical noise threshold to differentiate stochastic mini-batch sample variance from cross-task gradient conflict.
- **Leave-One-Event-Out (LOEO) Cross-Validation:** The 4-fold out-of-distribution evaluation protocol where all tweets originating from one specific Philippine disaster event are reserved exclusively for model testing, while tweets from the remaining three disaster events form the training and validation sets, repeated across all four disaster incidents.
- **Multi-Task Inference Throughput:** The processing rate of disaster tweets evaluated per second during batch inference on the testbed hardware, formulated as $\text{Throughput} = \frac{N_{\text{eval}}}{\sum_{b=1}^B t_{\text{batch}, b}}$ where $N_{\text{eval}}$ is the total evaluated tweet count and $t_{\text{batch}, b}$ is the execution time of batch $b$.
- **Multi-Task Optimization (MTO) Algorithms:** The encompassing suite of algorithmic balancing routines—spanning dynamic loss weighting methods and gradient surgery techniques—implemented in the PyTorch training loop to adjust task loss coefficients $w_k^{(t)}$ or project shared parameter gradient vectors $\mathbf{g}_k = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_k$ to optimize shared encoder parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}$.
- **Multi-Task Taglish Disaster Tweet Corpus:** The curated and manually validated dataset ($N \approx 6,000\text{--}10,000$ tweets) collected across four Philippine disaster events (Typhoon Haiyan, Typhoon Vamco, Typhoon Rai, and Typhoon Paeng), annotated concurrently for BIO location and infrastructure entity spans, message intent categories, and urgency priority levels.
- **Named Entity Recognition (NER) Span F1:** The harmonic mean $\text{Span F1} = \frac{2 \cdot P_{\text{span}} \cdot R_{\text{span}}}{P_{\text{span}} + R_{\text{span}}}$ computed via the CoNLL (`seqeval`) standard, where a true positive requires exact matches on both the token boundary span $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ and the entity category $E \in \{\text{Location}, \text{Infrastructure}\}$ from subword-aligned BIO tag sequences, discarding non-entity tokens (`O`) to prevent metric inflation.
- **Negative Transfer:** The empirical condition where joint multi-task optimization of shared parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}$ results in a lower task evaluation metric on task $k$ than an isolated single-task model ($\text{F1}_{\text{MTL}, k} < \text{F1}_{\text{STL}, k}$), producing a negative relative transfer score $\Delta_m < 0$.
- **Non-Parametric Hypothesis Testing:** The distribution-free statistical evaluation framework, comprising the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Friedman test with post-hoc analysis, used to determine whether performance differences between Multi-Task Optimization algorithms achieve statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) across disaster event splits without assuming normality.
- **Operational Disaster Triage Web Application:** The functional software deliverable that integrates the fine-tuned multi-task model into a FastAPI asynchronous inference backend and an interactive responder map dashboard to demonstrate real-time triage utility during disaster response.
- **Pareto Optimality in Multi-Task Learning:** The multi-objective optimization state where shared encoder parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}^*$ cannot be updated to reduce the empirical loss on one disaster triage task without increasing the loss on at least one other concurrent task.
<!-- vale off -->
- **Peak Concurrent Memory Footprint ($M_{\text{RAM}}$):** The maximum resident memory in megabytes consumed during multi-task model inference and training batch execution, measured using `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()` for GPU VRAM and `psutil.Process().memory_info().rss` for system host RAM.
- **Relative Multi-Task Transfer ($\Delta_m$):** The composite performance metric formulated by Maninis et al. (2019) that quantifies the mean percentage improvement of multi-task model $m$ over isolated single-task baselines across all $K=3$ triage tasks:
  $$\Delta_m = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{k=1}^3 \left( \frac{\text{F1}_{\text{MTL}, k} - \text{F1}_{\text{STL}, k}}{\text{F1}_{\text{STL}, k}} \right) \times 100\%$$
  where positive values ($\Delta_m > 0$) denote successful multi-task knowledge transfer and negative values ($\Delta_m < 0$) quantify overall negative transfer.

- **Single-Task Learning (STL) Baseline:** The reference model architecture where three independent Transformer encoders and task heads parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_k = \{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{enc}, k}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{head}, k}\}$ are trained independently on task $k \in \{\text{NER}, \text{Intent}, \text{Urgency}\}$ by minimizing $\min_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \mathcal{L}_k(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_k)$, providing task performance baselines $\text{F1}_{\text{STL}, k}$ and establishing the linear inference latency baseline $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot T_{\text{backbone}})$.
- **Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient ($\rho$):** The non-parametric correlation statistic $\rho = 1 - \frac{6 \sum d_i^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}$ measuring monotonic relationships between logged gradient conflict metrics (mean pairwise cosine similarity and gradient norm ratio) and empirical relative multi-task transfer ($\Delta_m$).
- **Static Linear Scalarization (LS):** The baseline multi-task optimization method that minimizes a fixed, non-dynamic linear combination of task loss functions ($\mathcal{L} = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathcal{L}_k$), tuned via grid sweep search across fixed task weight vectors $\mathbf{w} \in \Delta^{K-1}$ without dynamic adaptation during training.
- **Uniform Equal Weighting (EW):** The default static multi-task baseline that minimizes the arithmetic mean of all $K=3$ task loss functions, formulated as $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \mathcal{L}_k$ where $w_k = 1/3$, producing an unweighted gradient sum $\mathbf{g}_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{sh}}} \mathcal{L}_k$ to update shared encoder parameters.
- **Urgency Classification Macro F1:** The unweighted arithmetic mean of class-specific F1 scores across all $|C_{\text{urgency}}|$ urgency priority levels, calculated as:
  $$\text{Macro F1}_{\text{Urgency}} = \frac{1}{|C_{\text{urgency}}|} \sum_{c=1}^{|C_{\text{urgency}}|} \frac{2 \cdot P_c \cdot R_c}{P_c + R_c}$$
  where $P_c$ and $R_c$ denote precision and recall for urgency tier $c$ predicted from the pooled sequence embedding $\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]}$.
