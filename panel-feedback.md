The study searches X through the X API using search terms and the days around each tropical cyclone. The post IDs come from X itself, which returns them with each matching post. |
| 7 | Review the terms of X on who owns the posts, whether users agree to the use of their posts, and whether the posts may be stored. | Added to Section 3.5 that users who post on X keep ownership of their posts. The study may also keep a copy of the posts it collects, but it checks X for posts that users have deleted and removes them from the dataset. |

Earlier years are excluded because the study classifies posts and extracts their key details rather than measuring how Taglish changes over time. Within 2026, January to November is used because fewer months may not give enough Taglish posts after cleaning, and December is excluded because the posts must be collected before labeling begins that month. |

| 12 | Cite the source of the 3,900 posts and justify why that number is used. | Revised Sections 1.8, 3.2, and 3.3 to base the size of the dataset on annotation capacity, following the resource-constraint justification of Lakens (2022). Section 3.7 then tests whether 2,500 posts are enough by training the single-task models on 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the training posts and checking whether the F1-scores level off before 100%. |

| 14 | Report the results as they are, without trying to prove that the model performs well. | Clarified in Section 3.6 that the study reports the results as they are. The hypotheses test whether the final model performs differently from the single-task models rather than whether it performs better. | 15 | State the accuracy score considered acceptable. | Added to Section 3.6 that the study does not set a minimum acceptable F1-score. A minimum score would have to come from earlier studies on the same kind of posts, but no earlier study reports scores for both tasks on Taglish typhoon posts. The study instead compares the F1-scores of the final model with those of the single-task models and reports the difference as it is. |
| 16 | Define what accuracy refers to. | Clarified in Section 3.7 why the study uses F1-scores instead of accuracy to measure how well a model performs each task. Accuracy is the share of all predictions that are correct and can stay high even when a model misses rare categories such as rescue requests or the few key details in each post. Unlike accuracy, the F1-score decreases with each of these misses and therefore shows whether a model catches the posts and details that disaster offices act on. |
