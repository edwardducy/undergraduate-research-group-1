| 16 | Define what accuracy refers to. | Clarified in Section 3.7 why the study uses F1-scores instead of accuracy to measure how well a model performs each task. Accuracy is the share of all predictions that are correct and can stay high even when a model misses rare categories such as rescue requests or the few key details in each post. Unlike accuracy, the F1-score decreases with each of these misses and therefore shows whether a model catches the posts and details that disaster offices act on. |

If they still disagree, a third annotator from outside the group with experience in disaster management or crisis informatics decides the final label based on the guidelines. The third annotator signs a form for each batch to confirm that every disputed post was reviewed. |
| 18 | Show the test plan for pilot testing the prototype and the F1-score formula. | Added a test plan for the prototype to Section 3.9. For the experiments, three runs of each candidate model may be too few for the statistical tests to detect small differences between optimizers, and training time and memory may differ on computers other than the laptop used. | 1 | Clarify that one model performs both tasks and only sorts posts and identifies key details. | Clarified in Section 1.1 that the study trains one model to perform both tasks, rather than combining two existing models. | 2 | Specify how many models are trained and how many times each. | Specified in Section 3.5 that the study trains 15 models, two single-task models and 13 multi-task models, one for each optimizer. Each model except tuned static weighting is first trained four times to find its best training setting, and tuned static weighting is trained 36 times because it also tests how much weight to give each task. Since results change slightly each time a model is trained, each multi-task model is then trained three times and each single-task model five times. | 4 | State where the studies behind the 13 optimizers were conducted, and whether they apply to the Philippines. | Added to Section 2.2 that the studies behind the 13 optimizers were conducted outside the Philippines, but their optimizers still apply to the Philippines. | 7 | Clarify whether training requires waiting for a new disaster or uses posts from past disasters. | Clarified in Section 1.8 that training uses posts from past disasters and does not wait for a new disaster. | 8 | State the practical use of the study and who benefits from it. | Revised Section 1.9 to make researchers the main beneficiaries.
